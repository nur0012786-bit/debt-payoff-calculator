UNDERSTANDING YOUR NUMBERS

How the Debt Payoff Calculator works

A debt payoff calculator estimates how long it may take to pay down your balances based on the information you provide. It can also show how an additional monthly payment changes your payoff timeline and total interest.

What you need to enter

For each debt, enter the current balance, annual percentage rate (APR), and required minimum monthly payment. If you can consistently pay more than the minimums, enter that amount in the extra-payment field. The calculator then models the balances month by month.

Why extra payments matter

Interest is generally calculated from the outstanding balance. When additional money reduces principal sooner, future interest can be lower. The exact savings depend on the APR, balance, payment schedule, and how your lender applies payments.

Snowball and avalanche methods

The debt snowball method directs extra money toward the smallest balance first. The debt avalanche method directs extra money toward the highest-interest debt first. This calculator lets you compare the projected timelines and interest totals using the same debts and extra payment.

Example

Suppose you have several debts with different balances and APRs. Increasing your monthly payment by $100 does not simply reduce the payoff period by a fixed number of months. As one debt is paid off, its required payment can be redirected to another balance. That is why a month-by-month payoff schedule is useful.

Important assumptions

This calculator is an estimate, not a statement from your lender. It assumes the APR and minimum payments you enter remain unchanged and that payments are applied as modeled. Real credit-card and loan terms can differ, including fees, changing rates, payment timing, and lender-specific rules.

FAQ

Debt payoff calculator questions

What is a debt payoff calculator?

It is a planning tool that estimates the time and interest required to repay debt using a balance, APR, and payment amount. It can also model additional payments.

Does paying extra reduce interest?

Paying principal earlier can reduce the balance on which future interest is calculated. The amount saved depends on the debt terms and how the lender applies payments.

What is the difference between snowball and avalanche?

Snowball prioritizes the smallest balance. Avalanche prioritizes the highest APR. The calculator compares the projected results using your own numbers.

Is this calculator financial advice?

No. It is an educational planning tool. Your lender's terms and your personal financial circumstances should be considered before making financial decisions.
